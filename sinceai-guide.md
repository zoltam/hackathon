## How judging and the live finals work

There are three separate decisions:

| Decision | Who decides | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top three in each company challenge | That company's evaluators, using its agreed criteria | Review Sunday 10:00–13:00; awards from 13:30. |
| Five finalists | Participant voting in sinceai.app | Sunday 10:00–12:00. |
| Overall winner | The finals jury, after the live presentations | Sunday afternoon; separate €5,000 award. |

**The five finalists come from five different company challenges: at most one per challenge. They are not simply the overall top five projects.** Each eligible builder gets 20 pairwise comparisons and never sees their own team's project. The public Judge Guide leaves overall scoring criteria and weights to the judges' briefing; it does not publish a final rubric. [Source: Judge Guide](https://sinceai.ai/hackathon-2026/guide/judges).

### If we reach the finals

- **All five finalist teams present live on stage at EduCity, Sunday 14:00–15:10.** A submitted video does not replace this presentation.
- Finalists are notified after voting closes at 12:00 and receive presentation instructions.
- Report beside the stage at **13:00**; front-row seats are reserved for finalists.
- Each slot allows **up to four minutes presenting, then up to six minutes of jury questions**. The schedule includes five-minute breaks between presentations.
- The jury selects the overall winner; the event ends around 15:30.

[Sources: Builder Guide](https://sinceai.ai/hackathon-2026/guide/builders), [Field Guide finals schedule](https://sinceai.ai/hackathon-2026/guide#schedule).

## Questions for our challenge company

Suggested team questions for the Friday briefing and Saturday Q&A:

1. Who has this problem, and what is the most painful part of the current workflow?
2. What would make a weekend prototype useful, and how will you evaluate it?
3. Which data and systems can we use? What must stay confidential?
4. What assumptions, constraints or failure cases should we test first?
5. When and how can we show an early version and get feedback?

Representatives are not continuously available. Collect questions and use the support route they provide. Aim to show an early working version during Saturday Q&A.

## Submission checklist

**Submit in [sinceai.app](https://sinceai.app), not Devpost. Any team member can submit; one entry per team.** The dashboard shows “Submit project”, then “Edit submission”. Edits are allowed until submissions close.

### Required

- [ ] **Challenge:** our Friday selection, prefilled; only organisers can change it.
- [ ] **Project name:** at most 80 characters.
- [ ] **Tagline:** one sentence, at most 140 characters; understandable in five seconds.
- [ ] **About the project:** at most 5,000 characters. Cover problem, solution, how we built it, obstacles and next steps. Put the strongest explanation first.
- [ ] **Built with:** comma-separated technologies. The first five appear on the vote card.
- [ ] **Repository link:** public or private. For a private repository, judges use the source ZIP.
- [ ] **Demo video:** public or unlisted **YouTube link**, ideally under two minutes. No MP4 / WebM uploads. Test playback while logged out.
- [ ] **Source ZIP:** one file, at most **100 MB**, containing code as it stands at the deadline. Exclude dependencies such as `node_modules` and data files.
- [ ] **MLH questions:** technology feedback, main GenAI tools used (or no GenAI), and whether we implemented a generative AI model / API.
- [ ] **Challenge terms:** review them; submitting accepts them. If ownership transfers, every member must personally tick the terms for their work first.
- [ ] **Confidential challenge only:** public-safe summary and confirmation that project name, tagline, summary and Built with contain no confidential data.

### Optional

- **Demo URL:** judges can use it; it is not shown to voters.
- **Pitch deck:** one PDF / PPT / PPTX; voters can open it from the vote card.
- **Supporting documents:** up to two PDF / PPT / PPTX / DOC / DOCX / TXT / Markdown files for judges.
- **Partner technologies:** tick those actually used and explain how in a sentence or two. Ticking Red Hat OpenShift AI or FIWARE also enters its award; supply the requested evidence.
- **MLH categories:** select any that fit; zero, one or several are allowed.
- **Domain name / members' schools:** optional MLH questions.
- **Gemini API project number:** leave blank unless a category we enter requires it.

Add files with the submission and check the form for other upload limits. Keep the repository README and setup instructions current while building.

## Demo and voting strategy

Voters compare two projects at a time. They see project / team / challenge, the full tagline, opening description lines with “Read more”, the YouTube video, an optional deck, and the first five Built with technologies. **Repository links and deployed demo URLs are not on the vote card.**

Suggested demo structure:

1. Explain who has the problem and why it matters in one or two sentences.
2. Show the product working within the first 20 seconds: user action, AI behaviour, result. Record the screen rather than presenting slides; cut loading time and pauses.
3. Explain business value supported by the prototype or evidence. Do not invent metrics.
4. Narrate in English, add captions if possible, and keep the whole video around / under two minutes.

Mock data, simulations and clickable prototypes are allowed; clearly identify what works, what is simulated and what is future work. Prepare for a live presentation if we reach the finals.
